![Portrait of Bharat Agarwal](/assets/journey/me.jpg)

Growing up, I never imagined myself working in the social sector. I wanted to reach the exalted heights of Corbusier or Foster, not the messy, grimy, social-realist world of informal settlements. But this is where your surroundings shape you. In the classroom I saw the value of doing research, and asked myself: how can I make an impact as an architect?

It seemed to me that the “creativity” would be much more meaningful if it affected people’s lives.

![Scenes from the site of CodeCamp in Rakkar, near Dharamsala](/assets/journey/hillhacks.jpeg)

*Scenes from the site of CodeCamp in Rakkar, near Dharamsala. Source: Sahil Thappa.*

At the same time, I’ve always wanted to learn how to code. Computer Science was the other love, the other curiosity of my life that I didn’t get to fulfil at university. To that end, I attended a code camp at HillHacks, where I met a lot of people from the maker community in India, got exposed to ideas like soldering and prototyping with Arduino, and in some ways expanded out from my perfectionist architect bubble.

![A techno-commune on an organic farm](/assets/journey/Screenshot-2019-02-25-at-1.12.17-PM.png)

![Life at the farm](/assets/journey/Screenshot-2019-02-25-at-1.13.09-PM.png)

Living in a techno-commune on an organic farm has been one of the most unique experiences of my life.

Bangalore is teeming with energy for new technology, and I attended a lot of talks on blockchain, privacy, and user experience. But ultimately, I felt like I understood the first ten minutes of these talks, and then would look forward to clapping at the end. It was what a friend of mine termed “the cheerleader syndrome” — where you’re enthusiastic about the impact of technology without really knowing how it works.

![A diagram from my Launch School notebook](/assets/journey/Untitled-11.jpg)

Launch School provided me with a solid, first-principles education in web development. Here’s a diagram from one of my notebooks showing the inner workings of a low-level Ruby server.

This motivated me to hunt out Launch School, a rigorous self-paced program for learning software development. I’ve had my head deep into pseudocode, inheritance, encapsulation, and state for the last twelve months. Alongside, I also got to work in the fun, exciting — and random — world of Burning Man.

Right now, I can write clean, readable code in Ruby. I’ve attempted a few problems from the Advent of Code set, and have been really satisfied with solving them.

![Nizamuddin](/assets/journey/42884998_10155783944766220_421284696295473152_o.jpg)

*The tangled wires and delicious food of Nizamuddin — a place I visit often, and a major influence on my ideas about urbanity. Source: Protyasha Pandey.*

By September, I’m optimistic about having the skills of a full-stack web developer. I’m hoping I can combine my two interests of technology and urbanity, and create work which shows the beliefs and values I’ve picked up on this journey.
